Spacetime · black hole lensing
About this view

A non-spinning (Schwarzschild) black hole in front of a made-up sky of stars, a galactic band and a few galaxies. Every pixel's light ray is traced backwards from the camera through the curved spacetime, exactly: if it falls into the hole the pixel is black (the shadow); otherwise it shows the part of the sky the ray reaches.

Everything you see follows from that: the shadow, about 2.6 times the horizon's size; the Einstein ring when a galaxy lies right behind the hole; second, fainter images of the whole sky squeezed into a thin ring at the shadow's edge; and the distortion of the sky as you move.

Drag to orbit, scroll or use the slider to change the distance (in units of the hole's mass M; the horizon is at 2 M), double-click to reset. Close in, the shadow covers most of the sky; widen the field of view to see past it. The camera hovers at a fixed distance (it isn't orbiting or falling) and can't come closer than 3.5 M.
