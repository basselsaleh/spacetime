About this view

Gravitational-wave strain on a slice through the source: the orbital plane seen from above, a side view through the orbital axis, or any tilt in between (the sketch in the view panel shows where the slice sits). Colour is r·h (the 1/r fall-off removed), divided by its peak (2,2) value in the orbital plane and multiplied by the gain, on a smoothly saturating scale.

In the orbital plane the wave is purely + polarised (h× = 0). h₊ > 0 (LAL convention) means space is stretched around the circle centred on the source and squeezed perpendicular to the plane. Off the plane both polarisations appear.

Inside the ring is the near zone, where this far-zone formula does not apply. Black discs: horizons where the slice cuts them, at true size (at least a few pixels); faint outlines: the parts of holes hidden behind a slice. Orbits are post-Newtonian, derived from the wave phase, and schematic in the last orbits. Click a slice to move the probe (the ring marker).

The 3D view shows the strain throughout a sphere around the source, ray-marched like a glowing fog: crests (orange, h > 0) and troughs (blue, h < 0) of the wave show as sheets, which for h₊ form the two spiral arms of the binary and move outward at the speed of light. Same normalisation as the slices (r·h, so distant sheets are as bright as near ones); the near zone is left empty. h₊ and h× are measured against directions that wind around the orbital axis, so the sheets meet along it; a narrow cone around the axis is faded out. To keep it fast, the volume sums the three strongest modes (the slices use all of them) and is drawn at reduced resolution, adapted to the frame rate.
