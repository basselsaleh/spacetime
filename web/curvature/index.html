About this view

Space around a black hole, at one moment, cut through the hole's equator. That slice of space is curved, and this sheet is the shape a surface in ordinary 3D space needs so that every distance measured along it matches the real distance in the slice (Flamm's paraboloid, 1916).

Near the hole there is more room between two circles than their circumferences suggest: the grid squares stretch toward the center, and the sheet turns vertical at the horizon (r = 2 M), where it ends. Far away it flattens out.

Only the sheet's own geometry is physical: lengths along it and its curvature, colored here. Its height and the direction "down" mean nothing; it is not a rubber sheet that things roll into, and it shows space, not time. (Falling comes mostly from time running slower near the hole, which no picture of space can show.)
